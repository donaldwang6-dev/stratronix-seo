Three forces are converging: tightening data protection laws, surging AI adoption in regulated industries, and the realization that cloud-based AI services cannot legally process the most sensitive categories of LATAM data.

What "Sovereign AI" Means in LATAM

Sovereign AI is the principle that an organization — or a nation — should be able to control where its data is processed, who has access to it, and under what legal framework it operates. For Latin American enterprises, this has moved from theoretical concern to operational requirement in the past 18 months.

Three legal regimes define the floor for sovereign AI in Latin America:

  • LGPD (Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados, Brazil, Law 13.709/2018) — modeled after GDPR, requires explicit consent and legal basis for AI processing of personal data. Sensitive data categories (health, biometric, racial, political opinion) carry stricter requirements.
  • Ley 1581 de 2012 (Colombia, Habeas Data) — one of the strictest in the region, requires authorization for any data processing and grants data subjects strong rights.
  • Ley 18.331 (Uruguay, 2008) — the oldest comprehensive data protection law in LATAM, has served as a model for the region and emphasizes data subject control.

Argentina's Ley 25.326 and Chile's Ley 19.628 (currently being modernized) round out the major LATAM regimes.

Why Cloud AI Fails Sovereign AI Compliance

Cloud-based AI services (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, AWS Bedrock) face a structural problem in sovereign AI contexts: when a Brazilian patient's record is processed by a US-based cloud AI service, that data crosses jurisdictional boundaries. Under LGPD, this creates several compliance gaps:

  1. Legal basis uncertainty — Cloud AI providers often rely on legitimate interest or contractual necessity, both contested under LGPD.
  2. Cross-border transfer restrictions — LGPD Article 33 requires specific legal mechanisms for international data transfers, which most cloud AI providers cannot easily guarantee.
  3. ANPD oversight ambiguity — Brazil's data protection authority (ANPD) has indicated that cloud AI processing may require additional safeguards.

The same applies — with varying specifics — to Ley 1581 in Colombia and Ley 18.331 in Uruguay.

The Hardware Alternative

On-premises AI hardware — inference that runs entirely on a device physically located within an organization's premises — solves the sovereign AI problem at its root. If the data never leaves the device, there is no cross-border transfer, no ambiguous legal basis, and no ANPD oversight ambiguity.
