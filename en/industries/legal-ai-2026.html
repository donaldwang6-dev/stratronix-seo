1. Why Cloud LLMs Are Banned at Most Law Firms

1.1 ABA Formal Opinion 512 (2024)

The American Bar Association's Formal Opinion 512 explicitly addressed generative AI usage by lawyers. Key points:

  • Lawyers must understand the benefits and risks of AI tools used in their practice
  • Client confidentiality obligations apply to AI tool usage
  • Lawyers must supervise AI outputs and cannot delegate ethical responsibility
  • Unjustified fees for AI-assisted work may violate reasonableness duties

Cloud LLMs make compliance with these duties difficult because:
(a) the lawyer cannot fully audit how the LLM processes data
(b) the provider's employees may have access to client data
(c) cross-firm data leakage is technically possible

1.2 GDPR Article 28 and EEU Country Variations

Article 28 of GDPR requires data processors to provide sufficient guarantees of compliance. Most cloud LLM providers' standard terms have been deemed insufficient by EU data protection authorities. PAA's no third-party processor model eliminates this concern entirely.

1.3 Chinese Lawyers Law and Data Security Law

Law firms in China face specific requirements under the Lawyers Law (律师法) and Data Security Law (数据安全法) regarding client confidentiality. PAA's on-premises operation within the firm's office satisfies these requirements by design.
